# Apartment adviser: the owner's starting policies

These are the owner's personal valuation preferences and assumptions, including their biases. Apply them as the owner's criteria; they do not need external validation to be teaching targets. Attribute them to the owner rather than presenting them as verified facts about a building or seller. Keep unknown details unknown. An explanation should identify the preference used and the supplied listing detail or reviewed image interpretation to which it applies.

## Personal valuation preferences

- Being closer to subway stations increases value to me.
- Being closer to a supermarket or hypermarket increases value to me; I prefer a hypermarket to a supermarket.
- A construction year before 1977 lowers my valuation because I associate it with earthquake damage.
- A construction year before 2000 lowers my valuation because I assume its electrical wiring is not modern.
- Ground-floor and top-floor apartments lower my valuation because I associate them with theft or building risks. A lower valuation is not automatically rejection.
- Included parking increases value to me because I expect to rely on a private car in Bucharest.
- Photographs showing no renovations should lower my valuation.
- Photographs showing old furniture should lower my valuation.
- Photographs showing decrepit condition lower my valuation.
- Photographs showing modern, usable furniture increase my valuation.
- Windows facing a main road lower my valuation because I expect noise.
- A nearby park increases my valuation because I value opportunities for physical activity.
- Trees around the building together with a floor below 4 increase my valuation because I associate that combination with better air quality. This preference requires both conditions.

## Applying my assumptions

- My pre-1977 and pre-2000 penalties above are subjective decision rules. Apply them without requiring a structural survey or electrical inspection first. Actual damage, structural safety, wiring condition and renovation costs remain separate unknowns unless supplied. My original earthquake description called it “7.5”; that magnitude has not been verified here.
- I expect older apartments may have too few outlets for modern appliances and electrical upgrades to be expensive. This is part of my preference, not an inspection result.
- My starting intuition was approximately two million cars for four million people in Bucharest. These figures are unverified context for my parking preference, not a model input or a checked demographic fact.
- I assume about half of listings are misleading because people are greedy. Preserve this as my skeptical prior when considering listing claims; do not turn it into an observed deception rate, a calibrated probability or an assertion that a particular seller is dishonest. This assumption does not define a numerical price discount by itself.
- Interpretations of renovations, furniture and condition from images remain inferences. A checked extraction does not turn hidden wiring, structural condition or repair quality into observed facts.

## Price comparison and teaching

Listing price is a source observation, separate from the personal price I teach. Never automatically train on listing prices as my preferred valuations. Describe an asking price as above, below or equal to my personal valuation, not an objectively correct market price. Large gaps in either direction mean I discard the listing; smaller gaps may inform considering or negotiating it. I have not set numeric thresholds or price adjustments yet. Learn their magnitude and tradeoffs from my reviewed EUR judgments and reasons; do not invent them from these directional policies.
